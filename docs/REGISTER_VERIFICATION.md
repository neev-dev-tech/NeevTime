# Verifying the statutory registers

**Two procedures: one for you, one for the compliance officer.**

NeevTime produces the muster roll, overtime register and leave register from
attendance it collected. The content is right — every figure traces to punches
in the database, and the calculations are tested. What has never been checked is
whether the **layout** satisfies an inspector, because form numbers and column
orders are set by each state's Factories Rules and differ: Form 25 in Tamil
Nadu, Form 29 in Maharashtra, others elsewhere.

That is a claim about a document, and it is settled by a person who has sat
through an inspection reading one month of real output. Until then, the honest
position is "the record is complete; the form may need laying out differently" —
which is what the product says on the page.

---

## Procedure A — what you do (about 20 minutes)

### A1. Generate one real month

**Attendance → Reports → Statutory Registers.** Pick a complete month that has
already ended, with the full workforce in it. Not the current month — a partial
month invites questions about missing days that are simply not over yet.

Export all three:

| Register | Retention required | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Muster roll | 3 years | one row per worker, one mark per day |
| Overtime register | 5 years | one row per worker per day with overtime |
| Leave register | 5 years | approved leave over the period |

### A2. Read them yourself first

Two minutes, and it saves the officer's time for the questions only they can
answer:

- Does every current employee appear on the muster roll?
- Do people who left mid-month appear, with `–` after their last day?
- Does any row show `?` for a whole run of days? That means the system was not
  collecting then, and you should be able to say why before being asked.

### A3. Send them with the gaps stated

Do not present these as certified forms. Send the three exports, this document,
and say plainly:

> These are generated from our attendance system. The content is complete; we
> have not verified the layout against the Form our state prescribes, and one
> field a strict muster roll wants — the worker's father's or husband's name —
> is not held in the system at all. We would like you to mark up what an
> inspector would object to.

Stating the two known gaps up front is not weakness. An officer who finds them
unaided will wonder what else was not mentioned.

### A4. Act on what comes back

Column order and headings are layout, and cheap to change. A missing **field** is
different: it means data nobody is currently collecting, and someone has to
decide whether to start. Send the marked-up copy back and it becomes a change to
the product, not a discussion.

## Procedure B — what the compliance officer does

*Hand this section, and one month of exports, to whoever knows your state's
Factories Rules.*

You are being asked one question: **would an inspector accept these, and if not,
exactly what is wrong?** Not whether the numbers are right — they come from
biometric punches and are tested — but whether the document is the one the Rules
require.

### B1. The form itself

For each of the three registers, against the Form your state prescribes:

- Is the **Form number and title** required on the page? Which one?
- Are the **columns the prescribed ones**, in the prescribed order?
- Are any **required columns missing entirely**?
- Are any columns present that should not be?
- Is a **certification or signature block** required at the foot? Whose?
- Is any **establishment header** required — factory name, licence number,
  address, period?

### B2. The muster roll specifically

- Are the **day marks** acceptable? Currently: `P` present, `A` absent,
  `L` leave, `H` holiday, `W` weekly off, `–` not employed on that date,
  `?` no data recorded anywhere that day.
- Does your state require different letters, or a different treatment of a
  half day?
- **Father's or husband's name** is not held by the system and cannot be
  printed. How strictly is that enforced in practice, and is it required on the
  muster roll or only on the wage register?
- Should workers who **left mid-period** appear at all? They currently do, on the
  reasoning that a muster roll for a past period is a record of who worked then.

### B3. The overtime register

- Is a **rate and computed amount** required? The system deliberately shows
  hours only, because it does not hold wage rates and a computed figure from a
  rate nobody entered would be a number somebody relies on.
- Is overtime required **per day**, or aggregated per week or per month?
- Is a **reason for overtime** a required column?

### B4. The leave register

- Are **leave types** required to be named as the Rules name them (earned,
  casual, sick), and do ours map onto those?
- Is an **opening and closing balance** required, or only leave taken?

## What happens to your answer

Layout corrections go into the product and every customer gets them. A missing
field becomes a decision about what to start collecting — which is a
conversation with whoever runs HR, not a code change.

Either way it converts "we believe these are compliant" into "a compliance
officer has read a month of these and marked them up", which is the difference
between a claim and a reference.
