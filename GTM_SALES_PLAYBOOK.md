# NeevTime — Software-Only Sales Playbook (No Hardware)

**Date:** 17 August 2026
**Context:** You cannot sell hardware. That removes the dealer-bundle channel and forces the software to be sold on its own merits — which is actually where the margin and the moat are. This playbook is built around one idea:

> **Sell the software layer for readers the customer already owns.**

ZKTeco/eSSL devices are installed in a huge share of Indian factories, warehouses, schools and offices. Their owners are paying per-device BioTime licenses, hitting free-tier caps, and getting no leaves/approvals/registers/reporting out of it. NeevTime is the replacement software that works with hardware already on the wall. No hardware sale required — the customer's wall is the proof.

---

## 1. Who to sell to (ICP by buyer need)

| Buyer | Their pain | What you sell | Motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Replacement buyers** — have ZKTeco/eSSL readers, frustrated with BioTime/UTime license limits | Per-device license cost, capped free tier, no leave/approval/register features | Software that adopts their existing readers ("keep your hardware, fix your software") | Demo + migration support |
| **Compliance-driven factories** | Inspectors require Factories Act registers; current process is Excel | Statutory registers + audit trail + reports (verified against a real inspection) | Consultative, reference-led |
| **Contract/staffing workforce managers** | Attendance across multiple client sites, contractor reporting, buddy punching | Mobile punch + geofences + (later) contractor entity | Partner-led via staffing agencies |
| **Greenfield SMEs** (no readers) | No attendance system at all; want mobile-based, cheap | Mobile punch + geofence SaaS, ₹25–35/emp/mo | Self-serve trial |
| **Offices/coworking** | Geofenced mobile punch, no hardware | Same as above | Self-serve |

**Do not** target: enterprises with SAP/Workday already (they won't switch), or buyers who demand native iOS/Android apps (out of scope until Phase 1.5).

## 2. Where to sell (channels, ranked for a software-only founder)

### Channel 1 — Own website + SEO/content (highest ROI, start here)
This is your primary channel because the niche keywords are winnable and the compliance angle is a moat.

- **Landing page** with the wedge message: "Better software for the ZKTeco readers you already own" + pricing + 14-day trial.
- **Content targets (10 articles to start):** "factory attendance register format India", "ZKTeco software without per-device license", "attendance software for PF/ESI compliance", "biometric attendance software for factories", "how to maintain statutory registers", "geofence attendance for field staff", "attendance regularization best practices", "contract workforce attendance tracking", "ZKTeco UTime vs alternatives", "attendance software for small factory 50-200 employees".
- Each article ends with a trial CTA. SEO payback is 3–6 months; start now because it compounds.

### Channel 2 — Software directories & review sites (cheap, immediate discovery)
Indian SMB buyers discover software through directories and reviews. SoftwareSuggest is explicitly regional/SMB-focused with free submission (saashunt.ai, Apr 2026); G2, Capterra and GetApp cover global/enterprise buyers.

- **Do:** list on SoftwareSuggest, G2, Capterra, GetApp, SaaSworthy. Claim profiles, upload screenshots, publish pricing, gather reviews from the pilot site + any early users.
- **Reviews are the product:** 5–10 honest reviews from the pilot site make you credible. Ask for them the moment value is delivered.
- **Payoff:** directory traffic converts slowly but at near-zero cost and compounds with review count.

### Channel 3 — Software-only partners (replaces the hardware-dealer channel)
You can't sell through hardware dealers, so sell through *software and service* partners who already own SME client relationships:

| Partner type | Why they'll sell you | Deal |
|---|---|---|
| **CA / accounting firms** | They do payroll & compliance for SMEs; a registers+attendance tool makes them look good at inspections | 20–30% referral commission or white-label |
| **Payroll BPOs / outsourcing firms** | Same clients, same compliance pressure | Rev share per client |
| **Staffing & recruitment agencies** | They manage contract workforce across client sites — attendance is their biggest headache | Rev share + contractor reporting feature feeds them |
| **IT / web agencies serving SMEs** | They maintain SME websites; adding HR software is a natural upsell | White-label or commission |
| **Coworking & facility managers** | Geofence attendance for their members/tenants' staff | Commission |

- **Mechanics:** a one-page partner sheet (what it is, pricing, your commission), a demo account, and a simple referral agreement. Outreach: 30–50 targeted emails + LinkedIn messages to partners in manufacturing-heavy regions (Pune, Chennai, Coimbatore, Ahmedabad, Ludhiana, Bengaluru).
- **White-label** at +30% premium is attractive to IT agencies — your product is self-hosted, so white-label is technically easy.

### Channel 4 — Direct outbound (LinkedIn + email, demo-led)
For mid-size deals (300–1,000 employees) where a demo converts.

- **Target:** owners/HR heads/plant heads at manufacturing, warehousing, logistics, F&B, retail chains (50–500 employees).
- **Message:** compliance + replacement wedge, not "we're an attendance app".
- **Cadence:** 15–20 personalized messages/day → 10% reply → 2–3 demos/week → 1–2 closed/month is a good start.
- **Tooling:** free tier of a CRM/spreadsheet is enough at this stage.

### Channel 5 — Paid ads (only after content base exists)
- Google Ads on high-intent keywords ("attendance software for factory", "biometric attendance software India", "statutory registers software").
- **Budget reality:** US B2B software CPCs frequently exceed $20 (saashero.net, Jul 2026); India CPCs are much lower (typically a fraction — verify with a small test campaign of ₹10–20K/month before scaling).
- Run ads only when the landing page and trial convert; otherwise ads buy clicks that leak.

### Channel 6 — Launch & community (cheap credibility)
- Product Hunt launch (position as "open attendance for ZKTeco owners").
- MSME/business association meetups (CII, local chambers, industry WhatsApp groups).
- One talk/case-study write-up on the pilot deployment ("how one site replaced per-device licensing") — this becomes your sales asset.

## 3. How to sell (motion per segment)

| Motion | Buyer | Flow | Tools needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Self-serve (PLG)** | Greenfield SMEs, offices | Landing page → 14-day trial → email nurture → Razorpay checkout → onboarding checklist | Trial flow, billing, onboarding doc |
| **Partner-led** | CA/BPO/staffing clients | Partner refers → you demo → partner implements → commission paid | Partner sheet, demo account, referral agreement |
| **Consultative** | Factories, mid-size | LinkedIn/SEO lead → demo (registers + migration) → license quote + implementation fee + AMC | Case study, register samples, migration checklist |

**Pricing to carry (from the readiness review):**
- License: ₹40K–1.5L one-time per site (tiered by devices/employees) + 15–20% AMC/yr + implementation fee ₹10–25K.
- SaaS: ₹25–35/employee/mo (annual billing), 14-day free trial, floor ₹1,500/mo.
- Partners: 20–30% of first-year value (or white-label at +30%).

**Payments:** Razorpay (standard for Indian SaaS) for SaaS subscriptions; invoicing for license+AMC deals.

## 4. The 90-day plan

### Days 1–14 — Foundation (no selling yet)
- [ ] Land the wedge message: landing page (problem → screenshots → pricing → trial), domain, contact.
- [ ] Razorpay checkout + trial flow (SaaS tier) and a license quote form.
- [ ] Listings submitted: SoftwareSuggest, G2, Capterra, GetApp (free tiers).
- [ ] Ask pilot site for 5 written reviews (use them everywhere).

### Days 15–45 — Inbound + partner seeding
- [ ] Publish first 5 SEO articles (compliance + replacement keywords).
- [ ] Partner outreach: 30–50 CA/BPO/staffing/IT-agency contacts; one-page partner sheet + demo account ready.
- [ ] Google Ads test: ₹10–20K/month on 5 high-intent keywords, track trial sign-ups.
- [ ] LinkedIn: founder profile + 2 posts/week on compliance/attendance topics.

### Days 46–90 — Sales + prove
- [ ] 2–3 demos/week via outbound; convert pilot-site story into a one-page case study.
- [ ] First 3 paid customers (target: 2 license + 1 SaaS).
- [ ] Measure: trial→paid %, demo→close %, cost per lead per channel. Double down on the winner.
- [ ] Ask each new customer for a review + one referral.

**KPIs to watch:** trial sign-ups/wk, trial→paid conversion (>10% is good for this category), demo→close (>25%), CAC per channel, churn (SaaS).

---

## 5. What NOT to do

- Don't spend on ads before the landing page + trial exist.
- Don't chase enterprise RFPs/tenders yet — no references, no ISO/SOC2, no multi-tenancy decision.
- Don't build the native mobile app to "be competitive" — sell the web portal + mobile punch until a customer pays for an app.
- Don't claim "35 reports" — the backend implements ~10; fix the top 5 missing reports and market what works.
- Don't discount the license to "win" — the compliance moat and self-host justify the price; discounting signals weakness to a buyer who already owns hardware.
